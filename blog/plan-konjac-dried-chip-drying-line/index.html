How to Plan a Konjac Dried-Chip Drying Line

Plan the drying line as a connected process around the incoming material, required dried-chip output and factory conditions.

Published 2026-10-19 · Updated 2026-10-19

Direct answer

Begin with the incoming konjac material, the dried-chip result required and the conditions of the factory. ALL KONJAC presents a drying-line project as an integrated process covering material preparation, conveying, drying equipment, air handling and process integration. The right configuration is determined for each project rather than from an image or a generic capacity claim.

What belongs in the planning brief?

  • Incoming material: describe its form and any preparation steps before drying.
  • Output requirement: state the desired dried-chip result and target production output as project requirements.
  • Flow: map material movement into, through and out of the dryer, including existing equipment connections.
  • Factory: share floor space, access, utility availability and any installation constraints.
  • Scope: identify which preparation, conveying, drying, air-handling and support tasks should be included.

Images on the site illustrate equipment or process categories. They do not establish the exact equipment configuration, dimensions, energy use or achieved drying performance for a proposed line.

Questions for the project discussion

  • Which process stages must the supplier include, and which remain with the factory?
  • What incoming-material and finished-product information is needed to size the line?
  • How will existing equipment and available utilities connect to the proposed process?
  • Which installation, commissioning and training tasks should appear in the quotation?

Use the answers to request a project-specific process and equipment proposal. Confirm performance criteria and responsibilities in that proposal rather than inferring them from a reference page.
